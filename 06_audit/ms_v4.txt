New and Noteworthy
Calibrating an isolated sinoatrial myocyte model against heart rate measured in an intact patient attributes the baroreflex to the pharmacology and can invert the sign of a drug's apparent effect. We define an open-loop calibration framework admitting only autonomic-blockade or denervated-heart anchors, apply it to a 188-model population of human sinoatrial node myocytes, and show that a single anchor choice made on pre-specified design criteria moves a combination-risk estimate 2.3-fold. We further show that an endpoint defined as the fraction of a population crossing a steep threshold can manufacture apparent pharmacological interaction where the model contains none.
Abstract
Background. Combinations of rate-lowering drugs are widely co-prescribed, yet the sinoatrial contribution to combination bradycardia is essentially uncharacterised in humans. Combination rate-control trials are conducted in atrial fibrillation, where ventricular rate is governed by atrioventricular nodal conduction and the sinoatrial node does not contribute to the measured endpoint. Population-of-models simulation can address the question in principle but requires calibration against human observations, and almost all human observations are made with the autonomic nervous system intact.
Methods. The Fabbri human sinoatrial node model (1) was imported directly from CellML, validated against six published action potential features, and used to reproduce the population methodology of Zhou et al. (2). We define an open-loop calibration framework: because an isolated myocyte contains no baroreflex, only pharmacological autonomic blockade or cardiac transplant (denervated) observations are admissible anchors. Where several anchors exist, selection was governed by a rule fixed in advance on design quality, explicitly excluding the magnitude of the residual in vitro discrepancy as a criterion. A study-integrity rule prohibits any combination-derived quantity from setting any model parameter. The framework is demonstrated on verapamil and ivabradine.
Results. Baseline reproduction agreed with published values to within 0.99% across six features; retention was 188 of 940 models (20.0%, 95% CI 17.6–22.7%) against a reference 20.9%. Quiescence thresholds were heterogeneous but steep for L-type calcium current (median 40.0% block, IQR 30.0–51.25%, range 10.0–80.0%) and shallow for the funny current (176 of 188 models never quiescent at 90% block). Three admissible open-loop ivabradine anchors implied 58.4%, 50.9% and 31.2% funny-current block. Applying the design rule selected the anchor carrying the largest unexplained in vitro discrepancy (250-fold) and moved the maximum excess absolute risk at 480 mg/day verapamil from 6.9% (95% CI 4.1–11.5%) to 16.0% (11.4–21.9%) — converting a threshold that no arm crossed with interval support into one crossed at monotherapy ivabradine exposure. At 240 mg/day no arm crossed 5% with interval support under any anchor in either state. The pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic contributions reversed rank between the two licensed verapamil doses under every anchor and both autonomic states. Bliss independence classification varied non-monotonically along a single exposure ladder for pairs in which the model contains no interaction of any kind.
Conclusions. Open-loop calibration is a precondition, not a refinement, for applying isolated-myocyte population models to clinical drug questions. Calibration anchor choice, rather than model structure, dominates the resulting risk estimate. Interaction classifications computed on threshold-crossing endpoints describe the endpoint, not the pharmacology.
Keywords: sinoatrial node; population of models; drug-induced bradycardia; model calibration; ivabradine; verapamil
1. Introduction
Bradycardia arising from combinations of rate-lowering drugs is a recognised clinical problem. β-adrenergic antagonists, non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers, ivabradine, digoxin and amiodarone are co-prescribed routinely, and regulatory labels for several of these combinations carry explicit warnings (3, 4). The mechanistic contribution of the sinoatrial node to that risk, however, is poorly characterised in humans, and the reason is structural rather than accidental.
Combination rate control is used where rate control is required, which is predominantly chronic atrial fibrillation, and that is where the combination trials were conducted (5, 6). In atrial fibrillation the ventricular rate is determined by atrioventricular nodal conduction of fibrillatory impulses; the sinoatrial node does not contribute to the measured endpoint. A sinoatrial model therefore has no claim on those data at any calibration quality. The studies performed in sinus rhythm typically report submaximal exercise heart rate in intact volunteers, which — for reasons developed below — is not a measurement of direct sinoatrial pharmacology. The human evidence base for combination bradycardia is consequently near-silent on the mechanism most often invoked to explain it.
Computational populations of models offer a route to that question. The Fabbri model made a human-parameterised sinoatrial myocyte available (1), and Zhou et al. demonstrated a calibrated population built upon it in which the same perturbation produced normal rhythm, bradycardia or pacemaking failure depending on the underlying ionic background (2). Drug effects have subsequently been simulated in the human sinoatrial model at the single-drug level (7). The obstacle to extending this to drug combinations is not the substrate but the calibration: an isolated myocyte model must be anchored to something measured in a human, and almost everything measured in a human is measured with the reflex arc intact.
That distinction is not a technicality. An isolated myocyte is an open-loop plant. A resting heart rate measured in an intact patient is a closed-loop output, in which direct sinoatrial slowing is opposed by baroreflex-mediated vagal withdrawal and sympathetic activation. The two are different observables, and the difference is large enough to reverse sign. In awake instrumented dogs, intravenous verapamil shortened sinus cycle length in intact animals — reflex tachycardia — while in autonomically blocked animals and in orthotopic transplant recipients the transient shortening was absent, cycle length lengthened promptly, and sinus arrest occurred (8).
Here we set out a calibration framework built around that distinction, together with the procedural rules it entails: an anchor-selection rule fixed on design quality, a pre-specified comparison-point rule, and a prohibition on any combination-derived quantity informing any model parameter. We apply the framework to a 188-model population of human sinoatrial node myocytes and demonstrate it on verapamil and ivabradine. Consistent with the Methods and Resources format, the drug application is a proof of principle for the framework rather than a comprehensive pharmacological survey. Two findings emerged that we consider transferable beyond this pair: the calibration anchor, not the model, dominates the risk estimate; and interaction statistics computed on threshold-crossing endpoints are uninterpretable as pharmacology.
2. Methods
Every numerical value reported in this manuscript was read programmatically from frozen output files by way of a 142-entry values manifest; none was transcribed from prose. The manifest maps each reported value to the output file it came from and the script that produced it, and accompanies the submission.
2.1 Model and provenance
Simulations used the Fabbri et al. model of the human sinoatrial node myocyte (1), obtained as CellML from the Physiome Model Repository (HumanSAN_Fabbri_Fantini_Wilders_Severi_2017.cellml, PMR exposure e/568). The file was checksummed before every run and is identified throughout by SHA256 9062dd65533092914e04e425e09a81209aae9b44b37adae223f5f8e68b2aa1ec; any mismatch aborts execution, since a silently altered model file would invalidate the baseline validation on which all later results rest.
The CellML was imported directly by Myokit (9). No equation was hand-transcribed and the model was not re-implemented in another language, removing the most common source of unreported divergence between a published model and its reuse. Integration used CVODE with absolute and relative tolerances of 1×10⁻⁸. Control conditions were verified present in the file rather than assumed: voltage clamp off (clamp_mode = 0), no acetylcholine (ACh = 0 mM) and no isoprenaline (Iso = 0).
Baseline validation. The unmodified model was pre-paced for 500 s to reach its limit cycle and biomarkers were measured over a subsequent logged window at 0.1 ms resolution. Cycle length was 813.42 ms against a published 814.0 ms (−0.07%); maximum diastolic potential −58.89 mV (+0.02%); overshoot 26.41 mV (+0.03%); action potential amplitude 85.29 mV (−0.01%); diastolic depolarisation rate 48.02 mV·s⁻¹ (−0.17%); and (dV/dt)max 7.47 V·s⁻¹ (+0.99%). The largest absolute deviation across all six features was 0.99%. Steady state was confirmed by a cycle-length standard deviation of 0.0386 ms across 11 consecutive beats. This gate is executed as a hard precondition and the pipeline halts if it does not pass.

2.3 State isolation and determinism
Myokit's pre() advances both the current state and the simulation's default state, and reset() returns to the default state. Without intervention each sampled model therefore begins from the previous model's steady state rather than from published initial conditions, making results depend on evaluation order. The pristine state is captured once at compile time and both the state and the default state are restored before every model. The effect of the correction was quantified by re-running the entire population with and without it.
Determinism was verified on the drug-simulation store, in which concurrent workers independently re-executed a subset of identical conditions.
2.4 Open-loop calibration framework
The Fabbri model is an open-loop plant: an isolated sinoatrial myocyte with no baroreflex, no circulating catecholamine dynamics and no autonomic feedback. A resting heart rate measured in an intact patient is a closed-loop output in which any drug-induced rate reduction is opposed by reflex vagal withdrawal and increased sympathetic drive. The two quantities are not the same observable, and calibrating an open-loop model against a closed-loop measurement attributes the reflex to the pharmacology.
The decisive evidence is experimental. Qi et al. gave intravenous verapamil to awake instrumented dogs in three conditions (7). In intact animals sinus cycle length shortened — reflex tachycardia. Under autonomic blockade, and in orthotopic transplant recipients, the transient shortening was absent, cycle length lengthened promptly, and sinus arrest occurred. The same drug at comparable exposure moves heart rate in opposite directions depending only on whether the reflex arc is intact. A closed-loop resting heart rate therefore carries no reliable information about the direct sinoatrial effect and can carry the wrong sign.
Accordingly, only open-loop comparators are admissible for calibration: pharmacological autonomic blockade, or denervated (cardiac transplant) recipients. Intact-patient resting or exercise heart rate is excluded by construction, including where such data are larger, more recent or more precisely reported than the open-loop alternative. The corresponding model state is Fabbri control (ACh = 0, Iso = 0). Where an elevated-rate comparator is required, the published Iso 1 µM state is used; that state is a rate match to a denervated transplant heart and not a mechanism match, since a denervated heart is intrinsically fast rather than β-stimulated, and results are reported in both states so that state-dependence is visible.
An unavoidable practical consequence is that open-loop anchors are scarce, old and often incompletely reported. Where an effect magnitude could not be recovered from the primary source, the drug was carried as an exposure ladder across its plausible range rather than collapsed to a single fitted value.
2.5 Anchor-selection rule
Where more than one open-loop anchor exists for the same drug, the choice between them is itself a degree of freedom that can be exercised toward a preferred conclusion. The selection rule was therefore fixed in the study configuration, in advance, on design quality alone: (i) within-patient crossover, each subject serving as their own control; (ii) a drug-free baseline measured in the same patients rather than a between-cohort contrast; (iii) prospective rather than retrospective or observational design; (iv) earliest post-transplant, and therefore least sympathetically reinnervated, giving the purest open-loop measurement.
The rule carries an explicit exclusion clause: the magnitude of the residual discrepancy between an anchor's implied potency and the in vitro value is not a selection criterion and was not used as one. All admissible anchors are propagated through every downstream result as a sensitivity band, and reporting the primary alone is treated as a protocol violation.
A pre-specified comparison-point rule governs how an anchor is matched to an exposure. For chronic steady-state dosing the comparator is the time-average free concentration over the dosing interval, AUC(0–τ)/τ × f_unbound; peak and trough values are reported alongside as a labelled sensitivity and are never promoted to primary. This is fixed in advance because a clinical exposure band commonly spans three-fold, and an analyst free to choose the comparison point within it can generally find agreement.
2.6 Drug mapping and its scope of validity
Drugs enter the model as a fractional reduction of the target conductance, applied as a constant multiplier for the duration of the simulation. Free concentration is converted to fractional block through a Hill relationship, and free concentration is obtained from total plasma concentration using a measured unbound fraction. For verapamil the unbound fraction was 10.4%, the Hill coefficient 1.09, and the L-type IC50 is reported under two charge-carrier assumptions. The first is a Ba²⁺-derived value of 198.7 nM; the CiPA ion channel panel reports 202 nM for verapamil under Ba²⁺ charge carrier (10), and 198.7 nM is the working value used here after Hill-consistent re-solution at the panel's stated conditions. The second is a Ca²⁺-corrected value of 47.3 nM, obtained by applying a measured IC50(Ba²⁺)/IC50(Ca²⁺) ratio of approximately 4.2 (11); Ba²⁺ removes Ca²⁺-dependent inactivation, and both agents preferentially block the inactivated channel (12), so a Ba²⁺-derived value understates potency at the physiological charge carrier. Both were propagated, because the choice determines where a given clinical exposure falls on the population failure curve. Species protein binding was verified rather than assumed: verapamil binding measured in dogs and humans in the same assay was 90.7% and 89.6% respectively (13), excluding a species pharmacokinetic difference as an explanation for any discrepancy between model and animal observation.
Scope of validity, stated as a limitation rather than an assumption. Static fractional block is not a model of either drug's kinetics. Both act state-dependently and on a timescale long relative to the pacemaker cycle. Verapamil is an inactivated-state blocker (12) whose recovery time constant of approximately 5–7 s (11) exceeds the pacemaker cycle length by roughly an order of magnitude, so block accumulates across beats. Ivabradine block is current-dependent, exerted preferentially as channels deactivate on depolarisation and relieved by long hyperpolarisations, which amplifies its rate-reducing action at high rates (14, 15).
We tested directly whether a single fixed block fraction could reproduce a drug's effect at two different rates, and it could not; that state-transfer analysis is reported in the supplementary material. Static block is consequently applied only at a single calibrated state and results are not transferred across rates.
The direction of the resulting bias is determinate and one-sided. Under use-dependent block, slowing the cell reduces the number of block-accruing transitions per unit time and lengthens the interval available for unbinding, so real block falls as rate falls — a negative feedback limiting the drug's own effect. Static block has no such feedback: it holds the fraction fixed at the value appropriate to the pre-drug rate and continues to apply it to a cell the drug has since slowed substantially. Static block therefore over-states the sustained effect, over-states the fraction of models driven below threshold, and every risk estimate reported here is an upper bound. Both drugs err in the same direction, so the biases compound rather than cancel. This matters most where the predicted rate reduction is largest, which is precisely where the endpoint is decided.
Chronic oral exposure ranges were taken from the regulatory label rather than from a single pharmacokinetic study: 240 mg/day, 35–164 ng·mL⁻¹; 480 mg/day, 125–400 ng·mL⁻¹ total plasma.
Extrapolating an empirical anchor to altered exposure. An open-loop anchor fixes block at one concentration only. To evaluate the same drug at CYP3A4-inhibited exposure, the in vitro Hill slope (n = 0.80) was retained and the potency re-solved so that the concentration–response curve passes through the empirical anchor at the clinical free peak concentration of 12.2 nM. For the primary anchor this gives a re-solved IC50 of 7.98 nM and arms of 58.40%, 70.97% and 77.17% I_f block at one-, two- and three-fold exposure; evaluating the re-solved curve back at 12.2 nM returns the anchor block to machine precision. The assumption carried is that the empirical and in vitro concentration–response curves differ by a horizontal shift only. The 250-fold discrepancy between the re-solved potency and the in vitro value of 2000 nM for human HCN4 (15) is unexplained, so there is no evidence that the shapes are in fact the same; if the underlying mechanism is trapped or use-dependent block, the slope need not be 0.80 at all. Slope sensitivity was therefore reported: across n = 0.6–1.2 the two-fold arm spans 68.0–76.3%, while the one-fold arm is invariant by construction because the curve is re-solved through the anchor.
2.7 Endpoint: excess absolute risk
The primary endpoint is excess absolute risk (EAR), defined per-model and paired: the fraction of the population that continues to pace under drug A alone and under drug B alone, but loses automaticity under the combination. It is a count of models for which the combination, and only the combination, abolishes pacemaking. Confidence intervals are Wilson binomial intervals on that count. Single-agent risk is reported alongside EAR at every exposure.
Ceiling behaviour must be read explicitly. EAR is bounded above by the fraction of models surviving both single agents. As monotherapy risk rises toward unity that surviving set shrinks toward zero and EAR necessarily falls — not because the combination has become safer, but because no susceptible models remain. A declining EAR at high exposure therefore indicates monotherapy risk absorbing the excess.
Denominator at elevated autonomic state. At Iso 1 µM, 20 of the 188 retained models do not pace in the absence of drug. The denominator in that state is therefore the 168 models pacing drug-free, so that the endpoint measures drug-induced loss of automaticity and not a pre-existing failure to pace. This conditioning is applied identically wherever Iso-state results are reported.
Bliss independence is reported as a secondary artifact demonstration, not as the primary null. Any endpoint defined as the fraction of a population crossing a steep threshold will manufacture apparent super-additivity from additive target occupancy: two individually sub-threshold blocks applied together carry many models across their thresholds at once, with no mechanistic interaction required. Rungs at which no model failed in any arm, and rungs at which the endpoint is arithmetically ceiling-limited, are excluded from classification as uninformative.
2.8 Study-integrity rule
The claim under test is that combination loss of automaticity can be predicted from independently calibrated single-drug mechanisms. That claim is testable only if the prediction is made before, and independently of, any combination observation. The following rule is recorded in the study configuration and is binding on all analyses:
No quantity derived from a drug combination may be used, directly or indirectly, to set or adjust any model parameter. This covers the 12 sampled conductances and fluxes, every drug block fraction, every IC50 or Hill coefficient, every free-concentration assumption, and the retention criteria. Combination outcomes are outputs of this study, never inputs.
Violation voids the endpoint. Permitted calibration anchors are single-drug open-loop observations and in vitro channel pharmacology; forbidden anchors include any combination outcome, any observed interaction classification, and any excess-risk, Bliss, Loewe or highest-single-agent quantity.
The rule is enforced mechanically rather than by intention: pair-prediction scripts take single-drug block fractions as fixed inputs and contain no optimiser, no target value and no inversion of any pair quantity; single-drug calibration scripts do not read any pair output file; and every block fraction in use is traceable to a named monotherapy anchor recorded in the configuration's calibration-provenance block. Comparing a completed prediction against a published combination observation is validation, not calibration, and is permitted, since no parameter is adjusted to it.
3. Results
3.1 Model and population validation
The Myokit-imported CellML reproduced all six Fabbri Table 5 action potential features (Figure 1A). Cycle length was 813.42 ms against a published 814.0 ms, and the largest absolute deviation across the six features was 0.99%, in (dV/dt)max. Steady state was confirmed by a cycle-length standard deviation of 0.0386 ms across 11 consecutive beats.
Of 940 sampled models, 539 produced a stable rhythm, 401 did not pace, and there were no solver failures. 188 models were retained (20.0%, Wilson 95% CI 17.6–22.7%), against the reference retention of 1046 of 5000 (20.9%) reported by Zhou et al. (2); the reference proportion lies within the interval (Figure 1B). Retained intrinsic rates spanned 61.0–99.6 beats·min⁻¹ with a median of 82.2.
Correcting the state-leakage behaviour of the simulation interface moved retention from 187 to 188 models. One hundred eighty-seven models were retained under both conditions, one moved from rejected to retained, and none moved in the opposite direction. Mean absolute cycle-length change was 0.909 ms but the maximum was 241.4 ms — the aggregate effect is negligible while individual models can move substantially, which is why the correction was applied rather than argued away. Across 65,424 drug-simulation records, 59,784 were unique and 5,640 were independent re-executions of identical conditions by concurrent workers; no condition returned a different value on re-execution.
3.2 Quiescence thresholds are heterogeneous, steep for I_CaL and shallow for I_f
Across the retained population, the median fractional L-type calcium current block abolishing automaticity was 40.0% (IQR 30.0–51.25%, full range 10.0–80.0%), with no model tolerating 90% block (Figure 2A). The eight-fold span of the threshold is the population heterogeneity on which the endpoint depends; its steepness in the region of clinical exposure is what makes any threshold-crossing endpoint sensitive to calibration.
Funny-current block was far better tolerated: 176 of 188 models (93.6%) never became quiescent even at 90% block (Figure 2B), only 66.5% of models could achieve a 10 beats·min⁻¹ reduction through I_f block at any level, and the population median maximum reduction attainable through I_f block was 15.9 beats·min⁻¹. Monotonicity of block against quiescence was verified per model rather than assumed; no non-monotone ladder was found for either current.
This asymmetry matters for interpretation. Ivabradine, acting on I_f, cannot abolish automaticity in most of this population at any block fraction. Its contribution to combination risk is to lower the rate of models that are then carried across their I_CaL threshold by the second agent, not to abolish pacemaking itself.
3.3 Applying the anchor rule selected the least model-favourable anchor and changed the conclusion
Three published open-loop ivabradine observations satisfy the admissibility criterion. Applying the pre-specified design-quality rule selected the 8-week within-patient crossover study in transplant recipients (16), implying 58.4% I_f block. The three anchors imply 58.4%, 50.9% and 31.2% block, and residual in vitro discrepancies of 250-fold, 172-fold and 61-fold respectively.
The exclusion clause is load-bearing here: the selected anchor carries the largest unexplained discrepancy of the three, and had discrepancy magnitude been permitted as a criterion the opposite anchor would have been chosen. The superseded anchor was simultaneously the most flattering to the model on the in vitro gap and the most conservative on the risk estimate.
The consequence is not marginal. At 480 mg/day verapamil in the control state, without any pharmacokinetic interaction, maximum excess absolute risk was 6.9% (95% CI 4.1–11.5%) under the superseded anchor and 16.0% (11.4–21.9%) under the design-selected anchor, with the intermediate anchor giving 12.8% (8.7–18.3%). Under the superseded anchor no arm crossed 10% with interval support; under the design-selected anchor the 10% threshold is crossed with interval support at plain monotherapy ivabradine exposure (Figure 3).
A single anchor change — same drug, same admissible design class, same model, nothing fitted — moved the headline from "no interval-supported threshold crossing" to "10% crossed at monotherapy exposure". All three anchors are propagated through every downstream result.
3.4 Risk is confined to the higher licensed verapamil dose
Chronic oral exposure bands, converted at a measured unbound fraction of 10.4%, gave free verapamil concentrations of 8.0–37.5 nM at 240 mg/day and 28.6–91.5 nM at 480 mg/day, corresponding under the Ba²⁺-derived IC50 to 2.9–14.0% and 10.8–30.0% I_CaL block respectively.
At 240 mg/day, no arm crossed 5% excess absolute risk with confidence-interval support under any anchor, in either state. Under the primary anchor the maximum was 4.3% (2.2–8.2%) without pharmacokinetic interaction and 5.9% (3.3–10.2%) with a two-fold interaction, the latter crossing 5% on the point estimate only.
At 480 mg/day the picture differs. Under the primary anchor, maxima were 16.0% (11.4–21.9%) without interaction, 18.6% (13.7–24.8%) at two-fold and 18.1% (13.2–24.2%) at three-fold, all crossing both 5% and 10% with interval support, and all occurring at 27.5% I_CaL block — a free concentration lying inside the 480 mg/day band. In the Iso state, with the denominator conditioned on the 168 models pacing drug-free, corresponding maxima were 12.5% (8.3–18.4%), 14.3% (9.8–20.4%) and 19.0% (13.8–25.7%).
The Ca²⁺-corrected IC50 was propagated in parallel but is reported as self-falsifying rather than as a live alternative: the same mapping places verapamil monotherapy at 36.6–67.2% block across the 480 mg/day band, implying loss of automaticity in a majority of the population on standard chronic dosing, which the clinical safety record contradicts before any combination question arises.
3.5 Bliss independence is not a usable null on a threshold endpoint
Four pairs were classified under Bliss independence after excluding uninformative rungs. Classification was not invariant to exposure for two of the four. Diltiazem × verapamil, which act on the same channel and therefore contain no mechanistic interaction whatsoever in the model, moved between additive and super-additive across six informative rungs. Diltiazem × ivabradine, acting on different channels, moved between sub-additive and additive across seven informative rungs. Verapamil × mexiletine classified as super-additive at all five informative rungs, and verapamil × ivabradine as additive at all four.
The interpretation is geometric rather than pharmacological. Because the threshold distribution is steep (Figure 2A), two individually sub-threshold blocks applied together carry many models across their thresholds simultaneously, and the resulting population failure fraction departs from Bliss expectation without any interaction being present. The departure is a property of the endpoint's construction, and its sign and magnitude depend on where the exposures sit relative to the threshold distribution. Interaction classification is therefore reported only as an artifact demonstration (Figure 4), with excess absolute risk and single-agent risk used as the primary readout.
3.6 Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic contributions swap rank across the licensed dose range
Decomposing the combination into its pharmacokinetic component (CYP3A4-mediated elevation of ivabradine exposure at fixed verapamil block) and its pharmacodynamic component (verapamil's own I_CaL block at fixed ivabradine exposure) showed that neither dominates across the licensed range, and that the rank of the two reverses between the licensed doses.
In the control state under the primary anchor, at the 240 mg/day time-average exposure (2.9% I_CaL block), the pharmacokinetic contribution was −2.60 beats·min⁻¹ against a pharmacodynamic contribution of −2.26. At 480 mg/day peak (30.0% block) the relationship inverted: −18.29 pharmacodynamic against −2.60 pharmacokinetic, a ratio of approximately seven to one. The reversal was present under all three anchors and in both autonomic states.
The magnitude of the low-dose leg is anchor-dependent even though its direction is not. Under the superseded anchor the pharmacokinetic contribution exceeded the pharmacodynamic by 27%; under the primary anchor the margin is 15%, both contributions having grown with the pharmacodynamic term growing proportionally more. At 240 mg/day in the control state the pharmacokinetic contribution is therefore best described as the larger of two comparable terms rather than as dominant. The Iso state is more lopsided in the same direction, with the pharmacokinetic contribution exceeding the pharmacodynamic approximately 3.8-fold at the low dose (−4.43 against −1.17); the two states agree on the direction of the low-dose leg while disagreeing on how pronounced it is.
On the risk endpoint the pharmacokinetic arm is leveraged out of proportion to its rate effect, because excess absolute risk counts threshold crossings and in a heterogeneous population a small additional rate reduction tips models already close to their quiescence threshold. A contribution that is small on a continuous endpoint can be large on a threshold endpoint. The direction of this effect follows from population heterogeneity and invokes no independence null; its magnitude depends on the threshold distribution being correct.
A result that did not survive the anchor switch. Under the superseded anchor, a bounding arm corresponding to strong CYP3A4 inhibition was the only arm to clear a threshold with interval support in either exposure band, while every moderate-inhibition arm fell below every interval-supported threshold — a gradient corresponding to the distinction drawn in regulatory labelling between moderate inhibitors, for which co-administration is to be avoided, and strong inhibitors, for which it is contraindicated (3, 4). That correspondence does not hold under the primary anchor: the one-fold arm at 480 mg/day reaches 16.0% (11.4–21.9%) and clears 10% with interval support without any pharmacokinetic interaction at all (§3.3, §3.4). We report the earlier observation because its disappearance is itself informative — it is a second instance, on a different quantity, of a conclusion determined by the calibration anchor rather than by the model. The strong-inhibition bounding arm was run only for the superseded anchor and no equivalent arm exists for the other two, so the comparison cannot be repeated at the primary anchor without further simulation.
3.7 External comparison, and why it cannot arbitrate the calibration
The European summary of product characteristics reports that co-administration of ivabradine with diltiazem or verapamil produced a two- to three-fold increase in ivabradine exposure and an additional heart rate reduction of 5 beats·min⁻¹ (3). The matching model contrast is the two-fold arm at 240 mg/day, which contains both the exposure rise and verapamil's own effect.
At the pre-specified time-average comparison point the model predicted −4.44 beats·min⁻¹ in the control state under the primary anchor, a ratio of 0.89 to the reported value, and −6.19 in the Iso state (ratio 1.24). The observation lies between the two autonomic states. As a labelled sensitivity, the peak-concentration comparison point gives −11.24 beats·min⁻¹, over-predicting 2.25-fold; the corresponding over-prediction was 2.2- to 2.4-fold under every anchor. That an analyst free to choose a comparison point within a single licensed dose band could produce anything from 0.83 to 2.34 relative to the same observation is the quantitative case for fixing the comparison-point rule in advance. A trough comparison point was not available, as no 24-hour trough concentration is published for this dose; the lower edge of the band is itself the exposure-weighted average.
This comparison does not discriminate between the three calibration anchors, and we do not present it as support for the anchor choice. All three anchors give ratios of 0.83–0.91 at the pre-specified comparison point — a spread of 0.4 beats·min⁻¹ against a single reported figure with no stated variance. The reason is structural: the contrast is incremental, the combination at two-fold exposure minus ivabradine alone at one-fold, so most of the anchor's effect is present in both terms and cancels. Excess absolute risk admits no such cancellation, being an absolute count of models crossing a threshold, which is why the same anchor change moved the 480 mg/day risk conclusion decisively while moving this comparison by less than 0.3 beats·min⁻¹.
We had expected the recomputation to weaken agreement, since the primary anchor more than doubles the funny-current block; instead agreement improved marginally, from a ratio of 0.83 to 0.89, because the prediction had sat below the observation to begin with. We report the improvement without treating it as evidence, for the reason above.
The comparison is further limited in ways that are not resolvable from the source: the reported reduction is stated jointly for two calcium channel blockers at two exposure multiples rather than resolved to a single drug, is given without variance, and derives from healthy volunteers on a whole-organism endpoint. It bounds the model's magnitude loosely at the pre-specified comparison point; it does not validate it.
4. Discussion
The principal claim of this paper is procedural: open-loop calibration is a precondition for applying isolated-myocyte population models to clinical drug questions, not a refinement of them. The evidence is that the same drug at comparable exposure moves heart rate in opposite directions depending only on whether the reflex arc is intact (7). A closed-loop resting heart rate therefore carries no reliable information about direct sinoatrial pharmacology and may carry the wrong sign, and a model calibrated against it attributes the baroreflex to the drug.
This carries a practical cost worth stating plainly, because it will recur for anyone adopting the framework. Open-loop anchors are scarce, old and frequently incompletely reported; several relevant magnitudes could not be recovered from the primary literature, and the corresponding drug was carried as an exposure ladder rather than collapsed to a fitted value. That is the correct response to missing data, but it constrains how many drugs can be handled and is the main reason the present demonstration covers a single pair.
The second finding is that the calibration anchor dominates the result. A 2.3-fold movement in the headline risk estimate followed from choosing among three anchors that are all admissible, all open-loop and all defensible. We regard this as the strongest argument for fixing the selection rule in advance and reporting the full anchor band rather than a primary alone. The rule used here selects on design quality and explicitly excludes the residual in vitro discrepancy as a criterion; applied to these data it selected the anchor with both the largest discrepancy and the largest risk estimate, which is precisely the combination that would be indefensible had the rule not been pre-specified.
The third finding concerns endpoint construction. Bliss independence, Loewe additivity and related interaction statistics are defined on effect magnitudes. Applied to the fraction of a heterogeneous population crossing a steep threshold, they produce classifications that vary along a single exposure ladder in a model containing no interaction at all — including for pairs acting on the same channel, where no interaction is even representable. We suggest that population-model studies reporting combination effects should report excess absolute risk alongside single-agent risk, and treat any interaction classification computed on a threshold endpoint as descriptive of the endpoint rather than of the pharmacology.
A fourth observation concerns how external comparisons should be weighted. The single published combination heart-rate observation available for this pair agrees with the model at the pre-specified comparison point under all three calibration anchors, to within 0.4 beats·min⁻¹. That agreement is not evidence for any particular calibration, because the contrast is incremental and most of the anchor's influence cancels between its two terms, whereas the risk endpoint admits no such cancellation. An external check that is insensitive to the parameter under dispute should not be cited as support for resolving it, and we suggest that studies reporting agreement with a published combination observation state explicitly whether the comparison is capable of discriminating among the calibrations they considered.
On the specific pair, the finding is dose-localised. Under the primary anchor, excess risk at 240 mg/day verapamil does not cross 5% with interval support under any pharmacokinetic arm, whereas at 480 mg/day it crosses 10% with interval support even without a pharmacokinetic interaction. If borne out, the implication is that the hazard is concentrated at the higher licensed dose and is predominantly pharmacodynamic there, with the metabolic interaction amplifying rather than generating it. We state this as a model-derived hypothesis requiring clinical evaluation, not as a prescribing recommendation.
4.1 Limitations
Static block is not a kinetic model, and every risk estimate is an upper bound. Both drugs act state-dependently on timescales long relative to the pacemaker cycle (11, 12, 14, 15). We tested whether a single fixed block fraction could reproduce a drug's effect at two rates and it could not; static block is therefore applied only at a single calibrated state and results are not transferred across rates. The residual bias has a determinate direction: under use-dependent block, slowing the cell reduces block-accruing transitions and lengthens the unbinding interval, so real block falls as rate falls, whereas static block holds the fraction fixed at its pre-drug value. Static block consequently over-states sustained effect and over-states the fraction of models driven below threshold. Both drugs err in the same direction, so the biases compound. Every estimate reported here should be read as a bounded upper limit.
The ivabradine potency discrepancy is unexplained. The primary anchor implies a potency approximately 250-fold from published in vitro values (15). Use-dependent, trapped channel block is a plausible mechanism, but we have not demonstrated it, and the discrepancy is stated as an open problem rather than resolved.
Excess absolute risk is ceiling-limited by construction. It is bounded above by the fraction of models surviving both single agents and therefore falls as monotherapy risk approaches unity — not because the combination has become safer, but because no susceptible models remain. Single-agent risk is reported at every exposure so the two cannot be confused. Within the clinical exposure bands the endpoint is not ceiling-limited.
The model is a single myocyte. Loss of automaticity in an isolated cell is not sinus arrest in an intact node, which is electrotonically coupled, spatially heterogeneous, and subject to pacemaker shift and subsidiary escape. The endpoint should be read as susceptibility of the pacemaker cell population, not as predicted clinical asystole.
Autonomic state is represented by two discrete conditions. The model's isoprenaline representation is a binary switch, so a graded sympathetic axis was unavailable. β-adrenergic antagonists — the most widely prescribed rate-lowering drug class — are consequently not mappable in this implementation and were excluded. Extending the framework to a graded adrenergic cascade is the obvious next step and would open that drug class.
The external comparison is loose and structurally uninformative about calibration. It is a single value without stated variance, reported jointly for two calcium channel blockers at two exposure multiples, obtained in healthy volunteers on a whole-organism endpoint, and — as set out above — incapable of distinguishing among the calibration anchors considered.
The demonstration covers one pair. A scoping search of the combination literature — not a systematic review; it had no registered protocol, no reference hand-searching and no trial-registry component — found that combination rate-control studies reporting a heart-rate endpoint are conducted almost exclusively in atrial fibrillation, where the endpoint is atrioventricular nodal, leaving one pair for which both singles could be calibrated open-loop and a combination observation existed.
